\begin{abstract}
For $\lambda>1$ let $\lambda_{\min}(\lambda)$ be the bottom of the spectrum of Weil's quadratic form restricted to test functions supported in the multiplicative window $[\lambda^{-1},\lambda]$. The Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to $\lambda_{\min}(\lambda)\ge0$ for all $\lambda$. We prove two \emph{upper} bounds that hold without any hypothesis on the zeros of $\zeta$. Theorem~A gives $\lambda_{\min}(\lambda)\le C\lambda^{p}e^{-2\pi\lambda^{2}}$, with all constants explicit; for every $\lambda\ge\sqrt5$ one may take $\lambda_{\min}(\lambda)\le1.563\times10^{9}\,\lambda^{16}e^{-2\pi\lambda^{2}}$. Theorem~B improves the exponent to $e^{-4\pi\lambda^{2}}$ by means of Kaiser--Bessel windows. Both proofs rest on one observation: the image of the summation map $\varphi\mapsto\sum_{n}\varphi(nu)$ lies in the radical of the Weil form, and cutting such a function off to the window costs only a Gaussian tail. The exponent $4\pi$ agrees with the prolate spheroidal heuristics of Connes--Consani--Moscovici and with high-precision computations of $\lambda_{\min}$. We also observe that the prolate quantity $1-\chi_2(\lambda)\asymp\mu^{9/2}e^{-4\pi\mu}$ reproduces the certified numerical upper bounds of Zhu to within a factor of $20$, over $283$ orders of magnitude. This numerical observation suggests that the true decay rate has exponent $4\pi$. Theorem~A is formalized in Lean~4, on top of a formalization of Weil's explicit formula; the formal statement depends only on the standard axioms. These results concern upper bounds only. They say nothing about positivity and do not address the Riemann Hypothesis.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Weil's criterion on windows}
Weil's criterion states that the Riemann Hypothesis (RH) holds if and only if a certain quadratic form $W$ is nonnegative on test functions~\cite{Weil52,Bombieri00}. Connes and Consani~\cite{CC21} and Connes, Consani and Moscovici~\cite{CCM25} study the restriction $W_\lambda$ of $W$ to functions supported in the multiplicative window $[\lambda^{-1},\lambda]$. Write $\lambda_{\min}(\lambda)$ for the bottom of its spectrum. Then:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\lambda_{\min}$ is nonincreasing in $\lambda$;
\item RH is equivalent to $\lambda_{\min}(\lambda)\ge0$ for all $\lambda$;
\item numerically $\lambda_{\min}(\lambda)$ is positive but tiny, and the corresponding ground state is close to a prolate spheroidal wave function~\cite{CC21,CCM25}.
\end{itemize}
Proving positivity window by window therefore requires control of a margin that shrinks very fast with $\lambda$. This paper quantifies that shrinking from above, unconditionally.

\subsection{Results}
Put $\mu=\lambda^2$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:A}
There are effectively computable constants $C,p,\lambda_0$ such that for all $\lambda\ge\lambda_0$
\[
\lambda_{\min}(\lambda)\;\le\;C\,\lambda^{p}\,e^{-2\pi\lambda^{2}} .
\]
More precisely, for $\mu\ge5$ we have $\lambda_{\min}(\lambda)\le 2S\,B(\lambda)^2/c_0^2$. Here:
\begin{itemize}
\item $B(\lambda)$ is an explicit elementary function with $B(\lambda)e^{\pi\mu}=O(\mu^{4})$;
\item $S=\sum_\rho m_\rho(1+|\gamma_\rho|^2)^{-1}<0.0462$;
\item $c_0^2=0.00894$.
\end{itemize}
In closed form, for every $\mu\ge5$, $\lambda_{\min}(\lambda)\le1.563\times10^{9}\mu^{8}e^{-2\pi\mu}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:B}
There are constants $C,p,\lambda_0$ such that for all $\lambda\ge\lambda_0$
\[
\lambda_{\min}(\lambda)\;\le\;C\,\lambda^{p}\,e^{-4\pi\lambda^{2}} .
\]
\end{theorem}

Both statements are unconditional: no hypothesis on the location of the zeros of $\zeta$ is used. In terms of the half-width $a=\log\lambda$ of the window in logarithmic coordinates, $\mu=e^{2a}$, so the bounds decay doubly exponentially in $a$.

\subsection{Relation to previous work}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Zhu's conditional bound.} Zhu~\cite{Zhu26} proves, \emph{assuming RH}, the bound $\lambda^*(L)\le\exp(-Le^{L})$ for windows $[-L,L]$ in logarithmic coordinates, so $L=a$ and $\exp(-Le^{L})=\exp(-\tfrac12\sqrt\mu\log\mu)$. Theorems~\ref{thm:A} and~\ref{thm:B} remove the hypothesis and replace $\sqrt\mu\log\mu$ by $2\pi\mu$ and $4\pi\mu$.
\item \textbf{Zhu's certified numerics.} Zhu also gives certified \emph{numerical} upper bounds for $\lambda^*(L)$ with $0.5\le L\le2$, evaluated unconditionally on the geometric side, together with an empirical law $-\ln\lambda^*\simeq2\pi^2N(T^*)/\ln N(T^*)$. Section~\ref{sec:numerics} compares these numbers with the prolate quantity $1-\chi_2(\lambda)\asymp\mu^{9/2}e^{-4\pi\mu}$. The ratio stays between $3$ and $20$ for $0.5\le L\le2$, while the numbers themselves range over $283$ orders of magnitude. This suggests that $-\ln\lambda_{\min}=4\pi\mu+O(\log\mu)$, the rate of Theorem~\ref{thm:B}. Zhu's fitted law would instead behave like $2\pi^2\mu$ as $\mu\to\infty$. We stress that this is an observation about upper bounds only; the true asymptotics can be decided only with lower bounds, which are of the same strength as RH.
\item \textbf{Positivity on a fixed window.} In earlier work~\cite{Mori26} the author proved $W(f)>0$ on the window $[-\frac12\log5,\frac12\log5]$ with a Lean formalization. The present paper goes in the opposite direction: it bounds from above, unconditionally, the margin that any such window-by-window positivity proof can have.
\item \textbf{The CCM program.} In~\cite{CCM25} two steps remain open in the program:
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item the ground state is simple and even;
  \item the ground state is close to the vector $k_\lambda$ built from prolate functions.
  \end{enumerate}
  Our results concern neither step directly. The test functions of Theorem~\ref{thm:B}, however, are closely related to $k_\lambda$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Idea of the proof}
Let $\E(\varphi)(u)=u^{1/2}\sum_{n\ge1}\varphi(nu)$. If $\varphi$ is even and $\varphi(0)=\int\varphi=0$, then the Mellin transform of $\E(\varphi)$ equals $\zeta(\tfrac12+iz)$ times an entire function. Hence $\E(\varphi)$ lies in the radical of the Weil form: its Fourier transform vanishes at every zero ordinate $\gamma_\rho$, whether or not $\rho$ is on the critical line. This fact is classical~\cite{Connes99,CC21}.

Now suppose $\varphi$ is supported in $[-\lambda,\lambda]$. Then $\E(\varphi)$ vanishes for $u\ge\lambda$. Its restriction $k$ to the window differs from $\E(\varphi)$ only by the piece $r$ on $u<\lambda^{-1}$. By Poisson summation this piece is controlled by $\hat\varphi$ on $|\xi|\ge\lambda$. Since $\hat k=-\hat r$ at the zeros, the explicit formula gives
\[
|W(k)|\le2S\,\big(\text{size of }\hat\varphi\text{ beyond }\lambda\big)^2 .
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:A} takes $\varphi$ to be Riemann's function $h$, whose Fourier transform is itself and decays like $e^{-\pi\xi^2}$, cut off smoothly. Theorem~\ref{thm:B} takes a Kaiser--Bessel window, whose Fourier transform beyond the band edge is smaller by $e^{-2\pi\lambda^2}$.

\subsection{Formalization}
Theorem~\ref{thm:A} is formalized in Lean~4~\cite{Lean4,Mathlib} as a statement about the geometric side of the explicit formula (Section~\ref{sec:lean}). It builds on the formalization of Weil's explicit formula in the Lean library Zeta23~\cite{Zeta23}, which is released under the Apache License 2.0.

\section{Setting and conventions}\label{sec:setting}

Let $\lambda>1$ and $a=\log\lambda$. In the variable $y=\log u$, the space $L^2([\lambda^{-1},\lambda],d^*u)$ becomes $L^2([-a,a],dy)$. We use two Fourier transforms:
\[
\hat f(z)=\int_\R f(y)e^{izy}\,dy\ \ (\text{logarithmic variable}),\qquad
\hat\varphi(\xi)=\int_\R\varphi(x)e^{2\pi ix\xi}\,dx\ \ (\text{additive variable}).
\]
For a nontrivial zero $\rho$ of $\zeta$ with multiplicity $m_\rho$, put $\gamma_\rho=(\rho-\tfrac12)/i$. Then $|\operatorname{Im}\gamma_\rho|<\tfrac12$, and the multiset of the $\gamma_\rho$ is invariant under $\gamma\mapsto-\gamma$.

Weil's quadratic form $W(f)=QW(f,f)$ is given on the geometric side as in \cite[(3.19)]{CCM25}. Let $\lambda_{\min}(\lambda)$ be the bottom of the spectrum of its restriction to $L^2([-a,a])$ \cite[Thm.~3.6, Cor.~3.7]{CCM25}. Piecewise smooth functions supported in $[-a,a]$ belong to the form domain \cite[after (3.27)]{CCM25}. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rayleigh}
\lambda_{\min}(\lambda)\le W(f)/\|f\|^2\qquad\text{for every such }f\ne0 .
\end{equation}
Finally put $S=\sum_\rho m_\rho(1+|\gamma_\rho|^2)^{-1}$.

\section{A master proposition}\label{sec:master}

\begin{definition}[Hypothesis (H)]
A function $\varphi:\R\to\R$ satisfies (H) if:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\varphi$ is even, $C^1$, and piecewise $C^2$;
\item $\operatorname{supp}\varphi\subset[-\lambda,\lambda]$ and $\varphi(\pm\lambda)=0$;
\item $\varphi(v)=O(v^2)$ at $0$ and $\int\varphi=0$;
\item there are $\alpha,\alpha'>1$ with $|\hat\varphi(\xi)|\le C(1+|\xi|)^{-\alpha}$ and $|\xi\hat\varphi'(\xi)|\le C(1+|\xi|)^{-\alpha'}$.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

Given such $\varphi$, put $e(y)=e^{y/2}\sum_{n\ge1}\varphi(ne^y)$, $k=e\,\mathbf 1_{[-a,a]}$ and $r=e\,\mathbf 1_{(-\infty,-a)}$. Then $e=0$ on $[a,\infty)$, so $e=k+r$.

\begin{lemma}[Explicit formula]\label{lem:EF}
For real, compactly supported, piecewise $C^1$ functions $f$ with finitely many jumps, $W(f)=\sum_\rho m_\rho\hat f(\gamma_\rho)\hat f(-\gamma_\rho)$, and the sum converges absolutely.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $f\in C_c^\infty$ this is Weil's explicit formula applied to $f*f^*$ \cite{Weil52,Bombieri00}. In general, $f'$ is a finite measure, so $|\hat f(z)|\le C_f/(1+|z|)$ on $|\operatorname{Im}z|\le\frac12$. Mollify $f$. The zero side converges by dominated convergence, since $\sum m_\rho(1+|\gamma_\rho|^2)^{-1}<\infty$. The geometric side converges by dominated convergence (archimedean term), pointwise convergence (poles), and finiteness (primes).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Poisson summation]\label{lem:poisson}
Under (H), $e(y)=e^{-y/2}\sum_{m\ge1}\hat\varphi(me^{-y})$ for all $y$. Hence $e(y)=O(e^{(\alpha-\frac12)y})$ and $e'(y)=O(e^{\min(\alpha,\alpha')y-\frac12 y})$ as $y\to-\infty$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Radical]\label{lem:radical}
Under (H), $\hat e(\pm\gamma_\rho)=0$ for every nontrivial zero $\rho$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $\operatorname{Im}z<-\frac12$, Fubini and the substitution $v=ne^y$ give $\hat e(z)=\zeta(\tfrac12+iz)\Phi(z)$ with $\Phi(z)=\int_0^\infty\varphi(v)v^{-\frac12+iz}dv$. The function $\Phi$ is holomorphic on $\operatorname{Im}z<\frac52$, and $\hat e$ is holomorphic on $\operatorname{Im}z<\alpha-\frac12$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:poisson}. Both sides therefore agree on the connected set $\{\operatorname{Im}z<\alpha-\frac12\}\setminus\{-\frac i2\}$, which contains every $\pm\gamma_\rho$. Finally $\zeta(\rho)=\zeta(1-\rho)=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Zero-side estimate]\label{prop:zero}
With $A_0=\int_{-\infty}^{-a}|r|e^{|y|/2}$ and $A_1=|r(-a)|e^{a/2}+\int_{-\infty}^{-a}|r'|e^{|y|/2}$,
\[
|W(k)|\le2(A_0+A_1)^2S,\qquad S\le2\big(1+\tfrac{\gamma_E}{2}-\tfrac12\log4\pi\big)<0.0462 .
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By Lemmas~\ref{lem:EF} and~\ref{lem:radical}, $W(k)=\sum m_\rho\hat r(\gamma_\rho)\hat r(-\gamma_\rho)$. On the strip we have $|\hat r|\le A_0$, and one integration by parts gives $|z\hat r(z)|\le A_1$. For the bound on $S$, pair $\rho$ with $1-\bar\rho$ and use the classical identity $\sum_\rho m_\rho\operatorname{Re}(1/\rho)=1+\frac{\gamma_E}2-\frac12\log4\pi$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Poisson form]\label{lem:F5}
Let $T_0(s)=\int_s^\infty|\hat\varphi|$ and $T_1(s)=\int_s^\infty t|\hat\varphi'(t)|\,dt$. Then
\[
A_0+A_1\le B_\varphi(\lambda):=\tfrac32\sum_m\frac{T_0(m\lambda)}m+\sum_m\frac{T_1(m\lambda)}m+\lambda\sum_m|\hat\varphi(m\lambda)| .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[Master proposition]\label{prop:master}
Under (H), if $\|k\|^2\ge c_0^2$, then $\lambda_{\min}(\lambda)\le2S\,B_\varphi(\lambda)^2/c_0^2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:rayleigh}, $\lambda_{\min}\le W(k)/\|k\|^2\le|W(k)|/\|k\|^2$. Now apply Proposition~\ref{prop:zero} and Lemma~\ref{lem:F5}. The absolute value is needed because, without RH, $W(k)$ could be negative.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}}\label{sec:A}

Let $h(x)=\frac\pi2x^2(2\pi x^2-3)e^{-\pi x^2}$. It is a combination of the Hermite functions $h_0$ and $h_4$, so $\hat h=h$; moreover $h(0)=\int h=0$. Put $\delta=\lambda^{-2}$ and $b=\lambda-2\delta$. Let $\chi$ be the $C^2$ cut-off built from the smoothstep $S_5(t)=10t^3-15t^4+6t^5$: it equals $1$ on $|x|\le b$ and $0$ on $|x|\ge b+\delta$. Let $\psi=\frac{315}{32}x^2(1-x^2)^3_+$. Set
\[
\varphi_A=h\chi+\varepsilon\psi,\qquad\varepsilon=\int h(1-\chi),\qquad g=h(1-\chi).
\]
Then $\varphi_A$ satisfies (H) with $\alpha=\alpha'=2$, and $\hat\varphi_A=h-\hat g+\varepsilon\hat\psi$.

\begin{lemma}[Explicit tails]
Write $h^{(i)}=Q_ie^{-\pi x^2}$ and $U_i(b)=\sum_k|q_{ik}|b^ke^{-\pi b^2}$. Two integrations by parts, the bounds $\sup|S_5^{(j)}|=\frac{15}8,\frac{10}{\sqrt3},60$, and the Gaussian tail bound
\[
\int_s^\infty t^ke^{-\pi t^2}\,dt\le\frac{s^{k-1}e^{-\pi s^2}}{2\pi\big(1-\frac{k-1}{2\pi s^2}\big)}
\]
give $B_{\varphi_A}(\lambda)\le B(\lambda)$. Here $B(\lambda)$ is an explicit finite expression in $U_i(b)$, Gaussian tails, $\|\psi''\|_1=27.194$ and $\|(x\psi)'''\|_1=158.23$. Every term of $B(\lambda)e^{\pi\lambda^2}$ is bounded by a polynomial in $\lambda$ of degree $\le8$ times $e^{4\pi/\lambda}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Lower bound for $\|k\|$]
For $\mu\ge5$, $\|k\|^2\ge\int_{-0.1}^{0.1}|e|^2\ge0.00894$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Ball arithmetic (Arb~\cite{Arb}) gives $\int_{-0.1}^{0.1}e_h^2\ge0.0093256$, where $e_h(y)=e^{y/2}\sum h(ne^y)$. The perturbation $e-e_h$ is at most $4.5\times10^{-3}$ at $\mu=5$, and it decreases in $\mu$. Conclude by Minkowski's inequality.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:master} now gives Theorem~\ref{thm:A}.

For the closed form, put $F(\lambda)=B(\lambda)e^{\pi\mu}/\mu^4$. We show $F\le12298$ for all $\mu\ge5$.
\begin{itemize}
\item On $5\le\mu\le10^4$ we evaluate $F$ in ball arithmetic on $48{,}882$ intervals in $\lambda$. The Gaussian factors are combined with $e^{\pi\lambda^2}$ into $e^{-\pi(s^2-\lambda^2)}$, so that the enclosures do not blow up. The maximum is at $\mu=5$, where the point value is $12163.95$.
\item For $\lambda\ge100$, replace $b$ by $\lambda$ in the polynomials $P_i$ and bound $e^{\pi(\lambda^2-b^2)}\le e^{4\pi/100}$. This turns $B(\lambda)e^{\pi\lambda^2}$ into a Laurent polynomial in $\lambda$ with nonnegative coefficients and top degree $8$. Hence $F$ is nonincreasing there, and $F\le57.0$.
\end{itemize}
With $S\le0.046192$ and $c_0^2\ge0.00894$, this gives the constant $1.563\times10^9$.

As a check of the direction of the inequalities, $B$ exceeds the value of $A_0+A_1$ computed from the definitions by factors $80$--$140$ for $\mu=5$--$13$.

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:B}}\label{sec:B}

Let $\nu=2$ and $\beta=2\pi\lambda^2$. For $\alpha>-1$ put $\F_\alpha(s)=\sum_j(s/4)^j/(2^\alpha j!\,\Gamma(j+\alpha+1))$. Let
\[
\Phi_\beta(t)=(1-t^2)I_2\big(\beta\sqrt{1-t^2}\big)\mathbf1_{|t|\le1},\qquad
\varphi_B(x)=\frac{x^2(x^2-s)\,\Phi_\beta(x/\lambda)}{I_2(\beta)},
\]
where $s$ is chosen so that $\int\varphi_B=0$.

\begin{lemma}[Sonine--Gegenbauer]
$\int_{-1}^1(1-t^2)^{\nu/2}I_\nu(\beta\sqrt{1-t^2})e^{i\omega t}\,dt=\sqrt{2\pi}\,\beta^\nu\,\F_{\nu+\frac12}(\beta^2-\omega^2)$ \cite[Ch.~XII]{Watson}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Bessel bounds]
\begin{itemize}
\item $|J_\alpha|\le1$ for $\alpha\ge0$, and $|J_\alpha(w)|\le(w/2)^\alpha/\Gamma(\alpha+1)$ \cite[10.14.1, 10.14.4]{DLMF}.
\item For $\nu\ge\frac12$: $I_\nu(z)\le e^z/\sqrt{2\pi z}$ for $z>0$, and $I_\nu(z)\ge c_\nu e^z/\sqrt z$ for $z\ge1$, where $c_\nu=e^{-1}2^{-\nu}/(\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma(\nu+\frac32))$.
\end{itemize}
The last two bounds follow from the Poisson integral for $I_\nu$.
\end{lemma}

With $\omega=2\pi\lambda\xi$, the Sonine--Gegenbauer formula expresses $\hat\varphi_B$ through $\partial_\omega^j\F_{5/2}(\beta^2-\omega^2)$. Beyond the band edge $|\xi|\ge\lambda$ every $\F$ is evaluated at a nonpositive argument. The prefactor $1/I_2(\beta)\le c_2^{-1}\sqrt\beta e^{-\beta}$ then gives
\[
|\hat\varphi_B(\xi)|\le C\lambda^qe^{-2\pi\lambda^2}(\lambda/|\xi|)^{5/2},\qquad
|\xi\hat\varphi_B'(\xi)|\le C\lambda^qe^{-2\pi\lambda^2}(\lambda/|\xi|)^{3/2}.
\]
So (H) holds with $(\alpha,\alpha')=(\frac52,\frac32)$, and $B_{\varphi_B}\le C'\lambda^{q+1}e^{-2\pi\lambda^2}$.

For the lower bound on $\|k\|$, dominate $\Phi_\beta(x/\lambda)/I_2(\beta)\le36.14\,e^{-\pi x^2}$ using the bounds for $I_\nu$. Then $\varphi_B\to\pi^{-2}h$, and $\|k\|$ is bounded below as in Section~\ref{sec:A}. This last step is qualitative, so $\lambda_0$ is not yet effective. Proposition~\ref{prop:master} gives Theorem~\ref{thm:B}.

\section{Formalization}\label{sec:lean}

The Lean statement \lean{RhWeil.Concrete.theoremA} reads
\begin{quote}\small
$\exists\,C\,p\,\lambda_0,\ \forall\lambda\ge\lambda_0,\ \exists k:\R\to\C,$ $k\in C^2$, $\operatorname{tsupport}k\subseteq[-\log\lambda,\log\lambda]$, $0<\|k\|_2^2$, and $\big\|\mathrm{literatureRHS}(k\star\tilde k)\big\|\le C\lambda^pe^{-2\pi\lambda^2}\|k\|_2^2$.
\end{quote}
Here $\mathrm{literatureRHS}$ is the name used in~\cite{Zeta23} for the geometric side of Weil's explicit formula, in the standard form of \cite[Thm.~5.12]{IK04}:
\[
\hat g(\tfrac i2)+\hat g(-\tfrac i2)-\sum_{n}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\big(g(\log n)+g(-\log n)\big)+\frac1{2\pi}\int_\R\hat g(r)\Big[\operatorname{Re}\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\big(\tfrac14+\tfrac{ir}2\big)-\log\pi\Big]dr .
\]
For $g=k\star\tilde k$ this is the Weil quadratic form $W(k)=QW(k,k)$. As a numerical check of the conventions, take $k(y)=(1-y^2/a^2)^3_+$ and $\mu=5$. The geometric side, the zero side (the first $1000$ zeros) and a Legendre--Galerkin discretization all give $2.7877938966\times10^{-5}$, and they agree to about $10^{-12}$.

The formal proof follows a route slightly different from Section~\ref{sec:A}:
\begin{itemize}
\item the window cut-off is smooth, $k=e\eta$, so that no jump occurs;
\item Lemma~\ref{lem:F5} is replaced by a sup-type bound: if $|\hat\varphi|\le M/\xi^2$ on $|\xi|\ge\Xi$, then $|\sum_n\varphi(nu)|\le M\zeta(2)u$ for $u\le1/\Xi$;
\item the constants are existential.
\end{itemize}
The radical lemma is proved through Mathlib's Mellin transform and the identity theorem. Poisson summation is taken from Mathlib. The explicit formula is taken from~\cite{Zeta23}. The lower bound $\|k\|^2\ge c_0$ uses no numerics: every summand $\varphi((n+1)e^y)$ with $n\ge1$ is nonnegative. The development has about 3{,}300 lines. \lean{\#print axioms} reports \lean{propext}, \lean{Classical.choice} and \lean{Quot.sound}.

What is \emph{not} formalized:
\begin{itemize}
\item the identification of the Rayleigh quotient with the spectral bottom \cite[Thm.~3.6]{CCM25};
\item the explicit constant $1.563\times10^9$ (proved on paper with ball arithmetic);
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:B}, which would require a theory of Bessel functions that Mathlib does not yet provide.
\end{itemize}

\section{Remarks}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{What the theorems say.} Any proof of RH by window-by-window positivity must beat a margin that is, unconditionally, at most $e^{-4\pi\mu+O(\log\mu)}$.
\item \textbf{The polynomial factor.} It depends on the construction: $\mu^{16.8}$ numerically for $\varphi_B$, against about $\mu^{4.5}$ for $k_\lambda$.
\item \textbf{Relation to the CCM program.} The vector $k_\lambda$ of the program is of the form $\E(\varphi)$ restricted to the window, with $\varphi$ a prolate function. The mechanism of this paper therefore also bounds $W(k_\lambda)$ from above. Numerically $W(k_\lambda)\approx(8$--$14)(1-\chi_2)$.
\end{enumerate}
